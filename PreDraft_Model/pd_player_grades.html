Pre-Draft Player Grades

Every drafted QB, RB, WR and TE from the 2010-2026 classes, scored by the college-only pre-draft model, ranked within position and given a letter grade on one fixed scale. The top 2027 prospects (2026 season through about week 10) are slotted into the same rankings and marked as projected. NFL results sit next to every historical player so the grades can be checked. The post-draft player grades page grades the same 2010-2026 players with draft slot and landing spot added.

What the grades show

  • The grade sorts the top well and the bottom poorly. A+ players hit far more often than A, A more than B, and B more than C (69%, 53%, 31% and 16%). But C and D hit at about the same rate (16% and 13%), so a low grade means "the college numbers don't stand out", not "will fail". Many of those hits are late-round players who earned their role in the NFL (Kupp, Kittle, Tyreek Hill, Puka Nacua).
  • Draft slot predicts better than college stats alone. Among drafted players, pick number has an AUC around 0.81-0.87 against 0.67-0.78 for the grade. Scouts see film, size, speed and character, which this model deliberately leaves out.
  • The grade adds little on top of draft slot, except for QBs. In a logistic model with log(pick), adding the grade improves the QB16 and QB24 fit (likelihood-ratio p = 0.018 and 0.004). For RB, WR and TE the gain is not significant. Use the grade before the draft, or as a tiebreaker, not as a replacement for where a player was picked.
  • Within a round, a good grade still helps. Among Rounds 4-7 picks, A+ and A RBs, WRs and TEs hit at 10-33% versus 4-11% for C and below (table below).

The grade scale

Score = expected tier points = 3 × P(top tier) + 2 × P(middle tier) + 1 × P(bottom tier), which equals P(top) + P(top two) + P(any tier). The grade is the percentile of that score among drafted players at the same position in 2010-2023. The cut points never move, so a B in 2012 means the same as a B in 2027. There are five grades: A+ is the top 5%, A the next 10%, B the next 20%, C the middle 40% and D the bottom 25%. Until November 2026 there were eleven grades (A+ to F); a test of real outcomes showed only these five groups behave differently, so the in-between grades were merged.

Tiers follow your cutoffs: QB, RB and TE top 8 / 16 / 24, WR top 12 / 24 / 36, judged on the best PPR finish in NFL years 1-3. Historical scores are leave-one-class-out (each class is scored by a model that never saw it); 2024-2027 use the final model trained on 2010-2023.

2027 projected prospects

The highest-scoring draft-eligible players in the 2026 college season at each position (top 12 QBs, 20 RBs, 32 WRs, 15 TEs on either version), plus every player on the community consensus 2027 list, tagged "community". All are ordered by the model, not by the community list. Grades use the same scale as the drafted players. P(declare) is a rough estimate from production, recruiting and years out of high school. It badly understates elite juniors, so treat it as a flag only.

Pro-rating raises counting stats for teams that have played fewer than 12 games; shares, efficiency and age do not change. The gap between the two grades is how much a player's case rests on volume he hasn't piled up yet. Re-run after the season with full stats.

  • Low-grade hits include players with thin college data (Wentz and Kupp at FCS schools before 2022, when only their games against FBS teams are logged), slow developers (Josh Allen, Kittle) and converted players (Tannehill played WR for part of college).

Method and caveats

  • Model: the pre-draft model's chosen version per position (elastic net for QB, RB and TE; logistic regression for WR), trained on every draft-eligible college player 2010-2023 with non-NFL players as misses. Inputs are college production, team shares, career totals, breakout timing, years out of high school, recruiting, Power 5, transfers. The FCS level-of-competition translation (2022+) is kept.
  • Coverage: drafted players need a college stats match. Some drafted players are missing (for example Cam Newton 2010 and a few backs cfbd never logged), so counts run a little below the full draft.
  • 2024 and 2025 outcomes are best finish so far (two and one NFL seasons); 2026 rookies are mid-season and have no outcome yet.
  • Hit rates per grade rest on 7-62 players per cell; the bootstrap intervals in the chart are wide, especially for QB and TE.
